The engine does the quiet work

Your DAC gets a dedicated line

Aideo talks to your sound hardware directly instead of routing through the Windows mixer, so your 96 kHz files play at 96 kHz and nothing quietly resamples them on the way out.

Loudness that stays honest

An EBU R128 normalizer evens out volume between tracks, and a silence gate holds gain during song intros so a quiet acoustic number never opens at blast volume.

Crossfades without stutters

The incoming track buffers on its own pipeline, so a crossfade never eats into the song that's still playing. Rapid skipping re-syncs artwork and lyrics instantly.

Headphones, pre-calibrated

Pick your exact model from Jaakko Pasanen's AutoEq dataset of over four thousand measured headphones and IEMs. Aideo writes the corrective EQ for you and guards headroom.

Hear the difference yourself

Press B anywhere in the app and Aideo switches between the raw decoded stream and the tuned signal with AutoEQ and DSP applied. Same track, both paths, your ears as the judge.

Raw path

Symphonia decodes your FLAC to 32-bit float. The samples go straight to the hardware clock. What the mastering engineer bounced is what arrives at the transducer.

Tuned path

The same decode runs through the biquad array: your AutoEQ curve, crossfeed, spatial imaging, all in 64-bit math, then out to the same locked hardware clock.

Everything on the shelf

  • Lyrics that translate live. Timed lyrics from LRCLIB scroll in sync, with one-click Japanese Romaji and real-time translation while the song plays.
  • Scrobbling built in. Log plays to Last.fm and ListenBrainz natively, no desktop scrobbler app needed.
  • Your library follows you. Settings and library state sync across machines through Supabase, stored locally in SQLite first.
  • A phone remote on your Wi-Fi. Scan a QR code, get transport controls and queue browsing in any mobile browser. Nothing leaves your network.
  • Stage mode and Zen mode. Fullscreen lyrics with ambient art, or just the album at maximum size, cycled visualizers included.
  • Storage handled for you. Automatic storage management watches your music folders and keeps the library index current as files come and go.
  • Stream helpers, verified. yt-dlp and ffmpeg are checked against official SHA-256 signatures before they ever run.
  • Gentle on the machine. Roughly 75 MB of RAM at idle, and the liquid background renders stop the moment the window loses focus.

Straight from the build

Fair questions

Why does a music player need Rust?

The audio thread cannot afford pauses. Rust gives garbage-collection-free memory safety, which means no stutter from a collector deciding to run mid-song, and CPAL plus Symphonia are battle-tested Rust audio foundations the whole engine sits on.

Will it read my existing music folder?

Point it at any folder tree. The SQLite indexer walks it, pulls metadata and art through MusicBrainz, and keeps watching so newly added files show up without a rescan.

Do streaming sources sound worse here?

Tidal Hi-Fi delivers lossless audio and plays back exactly like a local file. YouTube Music is lossy at the source, and Aideo labels those tracks honestly rather than dressing them up.

Who makes this?

Alirul, a computer science student, building it in the open as an education in desktop architecture and DSP, AI-assisted and said so plainly in the readme. It's MIT licensed and the source is on GitHub.
